BB1 Drive Endurance

Run 23 Nov 2026, 07:18–08:57 local (UTC+2)Board BB1, Blackbox AeroNode rev 0.0.2 with CM5Pack 2S, 5 Ah (label value)Written by LIMA, 25 Nov 2026

BB1 ran on its own pack for 99 minutes: a 92 km drive to the office and 25 minutes parked and indoors. It drew a steady 2.06 W (0.26 A), used 3.4 Wh, and went from 91.9 % to 83.3 %. Speed and road made no difference to the draw. At this rate a full pack runs BB1 for about 18 hours before the low-battery shutdown.

What the run shows

The draw does not depend on driving

Split by GPS speed, the mean pack draw was 2.04 W below walking pace, 2.04 W between 4 and 70 km/h, and 2.03 W above 70 km/h measured. Motion, vibration and road speed don't change BB1's power. The compute load sets it, and on this run the CPU was capped at 1.5 GHz repo. Draw stayed between 1.70 W and 4.08 W in 2-second samples, with 95 % of samples under 2.37 W.

About a fifth of the pack power never reaches the CM5

The CM5's own power monitor (its PMIC) read a mean 1.62 W on the Pi rails against 2.06 W out of the pack measured. The gap of about 0.44 W (21 %) covers the 5 V conversion and everything on the board outside the CM5: sensors, GNSS, IMU and the charger itself. These two numbers come from different instruments, and this run can't split the gap between conversion loss and the other loads assumed.

A cold start warms up in about 40 minutes

The charger chip started at 11.5 °C and reached 29.5 °C. The CPU went from 20.4 °C to a plateau of 47–48 °C after about 40 minutes, peaking at 50.1 °C measured. The warm-up didn't change the draw. Cabin air temperature wasn't logged, so the rise can't be split between the car heating up and BB1 heating itself.

The draw rose about 0.2 W once indoors

From minute 84, around when BB1 went indoors, the draw rose from about 2.0 W to 2.2–2.4 W, and the Pi rails rose with it. The GPS fix dropped out two minutes later. The most likely cause is BB1 joining the office Wi-Fi assumed. Nothing on this run isolates it.

The pack powered BB1 alone

BB1 wasn't connected to car power. The pack discharged for the whole run and no sample shows charging measured. These figures are the pack's endurance with nothing topping it up.

The low-battery shutdown powers BB1 off after the pack has spent 60 seconds below 3.1 V per cell (6.2 V for this pack), which leaves about 5 % repo. The estimate assumes the draw stays at this run's 2.06 W.

Both the percentage and the hours scale with the pack's capacity. The logger counts charge against the label value of 5 Ah, and BB1's pack has never been run from full to empty on a meter to check it assumed. A 10 % smaller real capacity means 10 % fewer hours.

All three were aligned on UTC and averaged into 30-second bins. The battery figures come from the charger's own ADC, not an external meter, so treat currents and power as about ±15 % repo. Pack voltage is the one figure that doesn't depend on the current measurement: the five-minute means at the start and end of the run were 8.045 V and 7.835 V measured.

Not covered, still open

  • The pack's real capacity. Every hour figure on this page rests on the 5 Ah label value. A full run-down on an external meter would settle it.
  • Draw at the full CPU clock. This run was at the 1.5 GHz cap. A heavier compute load or an uncapped clock will draw more.
  • Wi-Fi's share. Wi-Fi was on for the whole run. The step at minute 84 suggests it matters, but it wasn't switched off to measure it.
  • Cabin temperature. The BME690 only stores readings while an AeroNode recording runs, and none was running, so no air temperature was logged.
  • GPS gaps. The first 3D fix came 2.2 minutes into the run, and there was a 3D fix for 4,530 of the 5,029 seconds the log spans. Speed is missing in those gaps. Battery and power data are complete.
  • Car power. Charging from the car while driving wasn't tried. The board takes 5 V USB only, which caps charging at about 12 W in.
