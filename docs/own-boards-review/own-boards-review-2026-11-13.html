Our own AeroNode boards: LIMA's review

For Chris, ALFA and GOLF. Answers inbox/peter/2026-11-13-002. LIMA for Peter Bruce, 13 Nov 2026. Tags: [measured] I ran it · [repo] in the files · [fetched] from a cited source · [assumed] not established.

Short answer. I agree with GOLF's verdict: better than expected, and not ready to send to JLCPCB as it is. I found no new board-killer. The fast pairs are the best part of the main board. USB 3 and PCIe are matched to within 0.04 mm and are coupled over almost their whole length. A continuous ground plane runs under them, except at the connector and via fields.

The power stage is where I would hold the order. I agree with GOLF's battery, charger and via findings. I add one layout problem GOLF did not raise. The charger's coil sits 11 mm from the chip, so both switching nodes run on the bottom outer layer, through via arrays at each end. They cover about 45 mm² each there, and the system rail runs between them.

Two smaller things should be fixed before upload:

  • The factory folder has three different placement files and three parts lists. Two of them are stale.
  • No board file states its stack-up or its inner copper weight.

AeroAudio does what it says: a codec and cabin mic board. Against John's 21 Oct spec it meets §5.1 (the cabin mic) and nothing else, which ALFA states openly.

The bigger issue sits on the main board. Its J14 audio socket now has a third pin order: not BlackBox's J9 and not my audio board's J1. It supplies 5 V where my board expects the battery. Someone has to rule on which audio board J14 is for before either board is ordered.

1 · How I checked, and what I could not

The pack has no KiCad layout files, though the message said it did: alfa/ holds the PDFs, the JLCPCB files, renders and the inspector. So every layout number here comes from the Gerber X2 files. KiCad writes each track's net name and each pad's part and pin into them. I wrote a small parser for them (lima/docs/own-boards-review/tools/).

Positive control: its lengths match ALFA's own pcbnew numbers in data/board_data.json to 0.01 mm. For example USB3_0_TX 44.45/44.49 mm, RX 41.62/41.62 mm and PCIE_CLK 6.63/6.63 mm. [measured]

Reference planes were checked by rasterising the inner-layer pours at 25 µm and sampling under every 50 µm of each pair. Schematic points come from ALFA's PDFs, and part positions from the factory CSVs.

Datasheets used:

  • Raspberry Pi CM5 data sheet RP-008180-DS-7 [fetched]
  • ADI ADAU1372 Rev 0 and UG-807 (local copies)
  • JLCPCB's 6-layer page [fetched]

Not checked:

  • AeroImu and AeroVault, beyond reading GOLF on them.
  • Clearances at the TPS25730 (GOLF B14).
  • The charge-lock pump timing (B1a).
  • Thermal behaviour.
  • JLCPCB's own placement preview.
  • Anything on hardware: nothing exists to measure.

Like everything else in this pack, this review is by an AI agent.

3 · Main board fast pairs

Stack as drawn:

  • F.Cu signal
  • In1 GND (one region over 8,621 mm²)
  • In2 signal
  • In3 +5 V plane (8,474 mm²)
  • In4 GND
  • B.Cu signal

So F.Cu references In1 ground and B.Cu references In4 ground. In2 lies between ground and the 5 V plane. Of the fast pairs, only the Pixhawk's USB 2 and short stretches of Ethernet use In2.

Impedance class. The CM5 data sheet asks for 90 Ω on PCIe and USB, and 100 Ω on Ethernet [fetched, §2.3.1, §2.4, §2.2.1]. The board uses 0.14/0.15 mm for the 90 Ω pairs and 0.125/0.20 mm for 100 Ω. Those widths hold only on the thin-prepreg JLC06161H-3313 stack (GOLF B12), hence L4.

Reference plane. Ground lies under every fast pair for its whole length, except 1–3 mm at each end where the pairs cross the connector and via-antipad fields [measured].

PCIe P/N swap. Both data pairs are wired P to N at the M.2 socket on purpose: TX_P to pin 37, TX_N to 35, RX_P to 43, RX_N to 41 [measured]. The CM5 data sheet allows it: "You may swap the positive (P) and negative (N) line within a pair" [fetched, §2.3.1]. The clock is not swapped (P to 47) [measured]. On first power-up, check lspci -vv for LnkSta Gen2 x1 and a clean AER count.

AC coupling. None is needed on this board. The CM5 carries the PCIe TX and USB 3 TX capacitors, and the Hailo card carries its own TX capacitors [fetched, CM5 DS pins 122/124, 140/142; §2.3]. This matches sheet 3's note.

What carries over, and what I measured myself

The main board puts I²S on GPIO18–27 as one clock pair plus four lanes each way [repo, sheet 1]. With the codec as master, the CM5 must use RP1's i2s1 (the clock-consumer block). I measured that exact configuration on a Pi 5 on 4 Sep, codec as master at 48 kHz:

  • 8 channels out and 8 channels in at once, byte-exact.
  • All four SDO lanes toggling.
  • Negative control: at 2 channels, lanes 1–3 stayed dead.

[measured, lima/linux/adau1860-pi5/duplex/MULTILANE.md; ADAU1860 not 1372, Pi 5 not CM5, kernel 6.12.47]. So the lane plan is sound. GOLF's software note "Unchanged: I²S on GPIO18–22" is incomplete: GPIO23–27 are now I²S lanes too, which is why PPS had to move off GPIO23.

The ADAU1372 core supply, on both boards

GOLF E1 says C11 on REG_OUT/DVDD should be 10 µF. ADI's data sheet gives two numbers:

  • The pin table: "decouple DVDD to DGND with a 0.1 µF capacitor".
  • The bypass section: "Bypass each supply signal on the board with a single bulk capacitor (10 µF to 47 µF)" [repo, ADAU1372 Rev 0 p.39, local copy].

The on-chip regulator's load regulation is 6 mV/mA, which makes bulk capacitance worth having. So I agree with the fix. I would drop "the script misread ADI's figure": the data sheet itself is inconsistent.

My own audio board has the same 1 µF + 100 nF on CODEC_DVDD (C29/C30) [repo]. It is logged against my board to fix; it is not changed yet.

AeroAudio layout, spot-checked [measured]

GOLF's E4–E6 hold:

  • MIC_OUT is 48.4 mm of track for a mic 13 mm from the codec. Most of that is the test-pad stub.
  • XTALO is 18.3 mm.
  • C7/C8 sit about 6 mm from AVDD pin 19, C12 (CM) 5.3 mm from pin 12, and C23/C24 about 7 mm from the crystal pins.

The stack (F / GND / +3V3A plane / B) is sensible. The mic's sound port is a 0.6 mm non-plated hole under U3.

The decision this exposes: which audio board J14 is for

There are now three incompatible pin orders for the CM5-to-audio FFC:

  • BlackBox's J9.
  • My audio board's J1, which mates J9 (our n = J9 25−n).
  • ALFA's J14, which mates AeroAudio J1.

Beyond the pin order, the power and control lines differ:

  • My board takes the battery on one contact (it boosts to 8 V for the A2B bus, and budgets 327 mA normal and 606 mA worst case at 5.0 V, docs/h1-power-budget.md). J14 gives switched 5 V on two contacts.
  • My board needs CTL_SPKR and CTL_MIC. J14 has AUDIO_IRQ, AUDIO_nRST and AUDIO_WDI instead.

J14's two power contacts and its watchdog line are better than BlackBox's single fused contact. My board can run from 5 V: it is at the bottom of its stated input range. So converging on J14 looks feasible. It is still a ruling, and it should come before the main board is ordered, because changing J14 afterwards is a respin.

7 · Smaller observations

  • U1A/U1B (the CM5 sockets) in the CPL sit exactly on the centroids of pins 1–100 and 101–200 (69.0, −47.0 and 69.0, −81.0) [measured].
  • C17 (bottom) and C18 (top) share one XY on opposite sides, both decoupling the GNSS 3.3 V. Not a fault.
  • U15 PG and U16 PG are unconnected. Either could gate the CM5's PMIC_EN, which the CM5 wants raised only after 5 V passes 4.75 V [fetched, §3.1]. Optional; it would also cover GOLF B2.

8 · What did not work, and what I would not trust

  • Counting "vias near a pad" by radius over-counted where other vias of the same net sit nearby. My first pass said 6 vias at the CM5's 5 V pins. A picture showed 3, which is GOLF's number. Every via count above is from a picture or from ALFA's own table, not from the radius count alone.
  • JLCPCB's default inner copper weight is not confirmed: their page lists the options but not the default. L4 says "state it" for that reason.
  • The switching-node concern (L2) is a layout-practice judgement against TI's guidance, not a measurement. It could prove harmless. Nobody can know until a board runs beside its GNSS.
  • The I²S lane result is mine but was measured on a Pi 5 with an ADAU1860. The CM5 uses the same RP1, but that is an inference, not a measurement.
