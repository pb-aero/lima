BB1 Power Reference

Board Blackbox AeroNode rev 0.0.2 (BB1, CM5) Measured 17–23 Nov 2026 For the power section of our own AeroNode board Agent LIMA · source pb-aero/lima

What a week of running BB1 on its 2S 5 Ah pack taught us about the power stage, measured on the board itself, and what each finding means for our own board's power stage (USB-C → TPS25730 PD sink → BQ25798 → VSYS → 5 V buck → CM5). Both boards use the same BQ25798 charger, so most of this carries straight over.

measured ran it on BB1 repo in a schematic or our code fetched from a datasheet or vendor doc assumed not verified, treat with care

The short version

  • BB1 draws about 2 W on battery (0.25 A at ~8 V) on a normal drive: a full 5 Ah pack lasts about 19 hours. measured
  • Charging tops out at about 12 W in and ~1.2–1.4 A into the pack from a 5 V 5 A USB-C supply, because rev 0.0.2 can only take 5 V. measured
  • A flat pack charges slowly: below 7.0 V (VSYSMIN) the charger runs in LDO mode and only ~0.36 A reaches the pack until it climbs past 7.0 V. measured
  • Nothing in hardware stops the pack running flat. BB1 browned out at 5.2 V (2.6 V per cell) under load. Software shutdown now covers it. measured
  • There is no fuel gauge. State of charge comes from the charger's ADC, which reads charge current low at low currents. measured
  • Charger defaults matter. Out of reset the BQ25798 charges at 1 A, and its watchdog keeps putting it back there; firmware has to own the charger. measured

What our board should take from this

Ranked by how much each would have changed BB1's week. P1 items are design decisions for this revision; P2 are worth doing now; P3 are bring-up checks.

P2Charger defaults and watchdog Out of reset: ICHG 1.0 A, ADC off, 40 s watchdog. When the watchdog expires ICHG goes back to 1 A. We now disable the watchdog and set 1.5 A from a service. measured fetched Same chip, same behaviour. Plan the boot service from day one (or strap/default to safe values that suit the pack) so charge rate does not depend on which software is running.
P2Power button The CM5 PWR_BUT is wired, but AeroNode deliberately ignores the key (bump protection), so a press did nothing and holding it hard-cut power. Now a double press shuts down cleanly. measured repo Decide the button's job in hardware: placement away from cables, and whether a long hold should cut power. Keep the "double press = clean shutdown" behaviour in software.
P3Charge current ceiling ICHG 1.5 A set, but the pack takes ~1.2–1.4 A. No limit flag is raised (no IINDPM, VINDPM, TREG, OCP). Unexplained. measured At bring-up, measure charge current with an external DC clamp on the battery lead, not the charger's ADC.
P3BC1.2 detection on shared D+/D− J1's D+/D− go to both the charger and the CM5 USB host; every adapter reads "unknown adapter", so 9 V Quick Charge never triggered. measured repo With a PD sink this matters less. If BC1.2 is still wanted, keep the CM5's USB pull-downs off the charger's D+/D− during detection.
P3OTG back-drive J1 presents as a sink only (no Rp, no DRP controller). With OTG on, plugging a charger into J1 back-drives VBUS. repo If the port ever sources 5 V, let the PD controller own the role swap.
P3Drain while off The charger ADC left on drains the pack while off; the service now turns it off at shutdown. The halted CM5's own draw was not measured. measured Measure off-state current at bring-up and decide whether ship mode is needed for storage.

How each finding was measured

Draw on battery

The battery logger reads VBAT and IBAT every 2 s (EN_IBAT on, so discharge current is sensed in battery-only mode). The discharge sign was proven by unplugging USB on 19 Nov: IBAT went from +90 mA to about −550 mA within one sample. The 23 Nov drive gave a clean 97-minute record, 8.05 → 7.83 V at a steady 0.25 A, with no sags at speed. measured

Why charging is slow

Three separate limits stacked up on 19 Nov. First, a flat pack below VSYSMIN puts the BATFET in LDO mode. A 15-second test at VSYSMIN 6.0 V raised input from 5.55 to 8.3 W, and the 21 Nov recharge showed the same jump on its own at 7.0 V. Second, 5 V USB with the 1.83 A ILIM clamp caps input at ~9.5 W; overriding to IINDPM 3.0 A raised it to ~12 W. Third, the charge current stops at ~1.38 A even with ICHG at 1.5–2.0 A, and an IINDPM sweep showed input was not the limit (it only bit at ≤ 2.4 A). A 30-second watch of every latched flag found nothing set. measured

Running flat

On 21 Nov BB1 booted on a half-empty pack with no charger and ran 66 minutes until the 5 V rail dropped out at 5.2 V. The journal ends with no shutdown messages. On re-plug the charger started in pre-charge (~0.1 A) and moved to fast charge at 6.17 V, inside the datasheet's 5.96–6.22 V window. measured fetched

Charging while off

Suspected not to work (CC goes only to the CM5), then disproved: with the PSU plugged and BB1 off for 30 min, the relaxed pack voltage rose 91 mV and no input-loss flag latched. The PSU keeps 5 V on with the CM5 off. measured

What didn't work, and what is still open

  • 9 V Quick Charge. Implemented per TI's HVDCP procedure, but the Pi PSU is USB-PD, not QC, and BC1.2 always saw "unknown adapter". No 9 V was ever reached. A PD sink is the real fix. measured (a440b79)
  • Reaching 1.5 A charge. Swept ICHG and IINDPM and watched every flag; the ~1.38 A ceiling is unexplained and can't be resolved without an external meter. Peter chose to leave it. measured (95b5728)
  • The power-balance estimate of charge current reads ~1.1 A where the ADC and the voltage step say ~1.3–1.4 A, so it under-counts at high current. The logger now takes the larger of the two. measured
  • The low-battery shutdown has never fired on a real flat pack. Checked with a forced threshold and a dry run of the poweroff permission; the unplugged test was skipped. measured (f4577ab)
  • My own errors, corrected: I first named the VBUS TVS as an SMBJ12A (it is an SMBJ18A, e41a70f), and I first believed BB1 could not charge while off (disproved, ccc755a).
  • Not measured at all: the halted CM5's off-state current, the true charge current with an external clamp, and the pack's real capacity (5 Ah is the label).
