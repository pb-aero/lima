H1 earcup power budget on the A2B bus

LIMA for Peter · 23 October 2026 · two cups, 32 Ω and 150 Ω driver options · datasheet figures and stated assumptions, nothing measured

In one paragraph. Each H1 earcup now takes its power from the A2B audio cable, so the question is whether the AeroNode's battery can push enough voltage through two cups. Straight from the battery it cannot: at the bottom of the battery's charge the second cup falls below the A2B transceiver's 3.7 V minimum. With an 8 V boosted bus — now drawn on the audio board — both cups have about 3 V of margin at any battery state. Each cup then draws about 290 mW in normal use and up to about 460 mW at full volume, and the whole headset costs the AeroNode battery roughly 0.8 W typical, 1.3 W worst case. Driver impedance (32 Ω vs 150 Ω) hardly changes the power; it changes how loud the cup can get.

The schematics behind these numbers (the cups' A2B sub-node and the 8 V boost) are drawn in LIMA's repo and are under Peter's review; they are not yet reviewed by the team.

1 · Why the bus needed a boost

A bus-powered A2B node takes power from the same twisted pair that carries the audio. Every hop costs voltage: Analog Devices' own budget is 2.39 Ω per hop plus 0.242 Ω per metre of cable (four bias inductors, a protection resistor, the transceiver's return switch, connectors), plus a Schottky diode at each node. Cup L carries both cups' current; cup R is last in line and sees the least.

Fed straight from the 2S LiFePO4 battery, cup R drops below 3.7 V whenever the battery is under 5.26 V at worst-case load. It would fail safe — a browned-out cup falls back to passive aircraft audio — but the digital path would die while the AeroNode still looks healthy, and on the way down the speaker switch's LED current falls below its guaranteed operating level. The audio board now carries an LM2731 boost that holds the bus at 8.00 V. The speaker is not the big load. The A2B transceiver (~195 mW — its internal regulators burn the whole bus voltage), the DSP core (assumed) and the speaker-switch LEDs (~85 mW, on whenever the cup is in normal mode) dominate. A small pre-regulator for the transceiver would roughly halve its share; it is not needed to make the budget close.

3 · What the driver impedance actually changes

Loudness in normal mode. The codec's output is limited by voltage — 1.0 V rms full scale, which is its 30 mW rating into 32 Ω. Into 150 Ω the same voltage delivers only 6.7 mW, 6.7 dB less. A 150 Ω driver needs about 6.7 dB more sensitivity (dB SPL per mW) to match a 32 Ω driver at full scale; the drivers' sensitivities are not yet in hand.

Loudness in bypass. With the AeroNode off, the panel drives each speaker through an isolation transformer (Triad SP-74) wired for 32 Ω. At a Garmin GMA 340's full headphone output (100 mW into 150 Ω, 3.87 V rms) a 32 Ω driver gets 47 mW and a 150 Ω driver only 10 mW. For a 150 Ω driver the transformer secondaries should be wired in series: the driver then gets about 40 mW, the panel sees about 185 Ω for the pair, and each transformer stays inside its 50 mW rating.

4 · What is assumed, and what this does not cover

  • The DSP core current with the HiFi 3z running — the largest single assumption; ADI's published figure leaves it out.
  • The Schottky diode drop in the bus path at 0.1–0.2 A (taken as 0.38 V; +0.1 V costs 0.2 V at cup R on a battery-direct bus). The 8 V bus makes the conclusion insensitive to both.
  • Cable lengths: 2.0 m AeroNode-to-cup, 0.6 m between cups. A metre more costs only 0.05 V.
  • No transient analysis (PPG LED pulses, A2B start-up inrush — ADI recommends sizing the bus supply for ≥ 150 mA), and no measured boost efficiency.
  • Driver sensitivity for whichever 32 Ω or 150 Ω part is chosen.

5 · What did not work

The first version of the bus fed the cups straight from the battery, following the earlier audio-board design. Working the budget is what showed it failing at the bottom of the battery's range — and that the old board's A2B circuit had never connected the positive bus bias at all, so the cups would not have been powered over the cable. Both are fixed in the current schematics: the full bus-power switch is drawn, and the bus now runs from the 8 V boost.
