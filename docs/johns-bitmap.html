Widths, and why 24 and not 32

Rev G specifies A2B data slots at 24 bits, not the 32-bit default, on an ENOB argument: ADAU1761 ENOB ≈ 16–16.5 bits, ADAU1860 ≈ 17–19, against 24-bit transport's 144 dB of range.

“The transport is never the bottleneck; the converters are.”

A 32-bit slot would carry “8 LSBs of pure converter noise while consuming 33% more bus bandwidth per channel.”

Ruled 31 AugA2B slot size is DNSIZE=24, UPSIZE=24. Rev M recommended 32; John ruled 24 [L-2026-W36-001]. The decisive reason is codec-independence: the ADAU1797A documents a 24-bit serial-port input limit (Rev 0 p.90), the ADAU1860-1 documents no serial-port bit limit at all. At DNSIZE=24 the AD2428W truncates in the main node and zero-fills, so both candidate cup codecs receive 24 significant bits followed by eight zeros — harmless on the 1797A, and never invoking undocumented behaviour on the 1860-1. S32_LE with no format conversion and no dither is unchanged. This supersedes the earlier “widen the A2B slots to 32” direction in [L-2026-W35-016].
LinkFraming
CM5 ↔ A2B main node TDM8 at 48 kHz, S24_LE in 32-bit slots, MSB-justified, CM5 PCM in slave mode
A2B bus SLOTFMT 0x10 — DNSIZE[2:0] = 24, UPSIZE[6:4] = 24 (the two are independent). Widths of 20–32 bits require A2B_I2SGCFG.TDMSS set, so the TDM channel is 32-bit while the slot is 24 — the “24-in-32” shorthand was recording exactly this
Cup serial ports Rev M §4: TDM8 — the Rev G TDM8 figure is cup-side, and it survives
Clock ADAU1761 is system clock master off a local 12.288 MHz oscillator with the PLL active [L-2026-W36-002]; the AD2428W main-node PLL locks to SYNC and subnodes recover from the bus. One clock domain, no ASRC — and the domain survives a CM5 hang

Precision discipline

  • ≥ 24 bits wherever a signal moves — that is the converter depth and it is not negotiable.
  • 32 bits wherever a DSP touches it. Any 24-bit intermediate inside a DSP graph is “a defect.”
  • Exactly two sanctioned dither points in the whole system — the CM5 TDM out, and the ADAU1860 DAC.
  • Upstream has none. The mic uplink is routed from the ADC output straight to the serial port, DSP-bypassed, so it never enters a 32-bit domain.

John's stated direction on the CM5 side, when asked to choose: “I don't want to rely on the CM5 and dithering, would rather it stays as S32_LE.”

What the same document also fixes

Two things ride along with the bit map and are worth knowing, because they are safety statements:

  • Fail-passive analog architecture. Each cup's changeover relay routes an analog pair straight to the driver when de-energised. The coil is held by two conditions in series — A2B bus power present in the cup, and a CM5 heartbeat — so bus-power loss, a DSP hang or a dead CM5 all drop the headset to wired analog with no software in the loop. A failsafe source relay carries raw buffered radio RX through an always-powered buffer: “the pilot hears the radio in both ears… even with the CM5 completely down.” The failsafe leg carries program audio only — no ANC.
  • The AD2428W has no SPI interface at all — I²C control plus I²S/TDM audio. SPI would require an AD243x part and a board respin.
