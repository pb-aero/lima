## THE FAULT IS THE HARNESS, not the part `[measured]`

Peter shorted `SDI` to `SDO` at the click board's own header. The loop does not close.

```

POSITIVE CONTROL FAIL — no echo at any clock
```

And then the same question asked with **DC only — no SPI, no clock, no timing, nothing that can be

| MOSI driven | MISO pull | MISO reads |
|---|---|---|
| LOW | up | **hi** |
| HIGH | up | **hi** |
| LOW | down | **lo** |
| HIGH | down | **lo** |

### The discriminating measurement: reads are perfect, writes are not

Three raw passes over the same 4 MiB at 25 MHz, read-only:

```
pass 1: 1bd12a8ad8341f9d7f4ff1334946f8dd
pass 2: 1bd12a8ad8341f9d7f4ff1334946f8dd
pass 3: 1bd12a8ad8341f9d7f4ff1334946f8dd
```

**Byte-identical.** The link is not randomly corrupting traffic — reads are deterministic and
repeatable at the same clock the writes were failing at.

That asymmetry points at one wire. **A read puts about four bytes on MOSI** (opcode plus address)
and then listens; **a page program pushes 2048 bytes up MOSI at full clock.** A MOSI path that is
marginal at 25 MHz — a long unshielded flying lead with no ground plane beside it — passes every
short command and corrupts every bulk write, which is exactly the signature above. `[derived]`

### Two counters that look alarming and are not

1. **`bad_blocks` was 3.** `[fetched]` That is normal and expected: *"The W25N01GV may have initial
   invalid blocks when it ships from factory... Block 0, block address 00h is guaranteed to be a
   valid block at the time of shipment."* NAND ships with factory bad blocks; only block 0 is

2. **`ecc_failures` climbing during a raw `nanddump`** is expected too — dumping erased or invalid

**Write protection is also ruled out** `[fetched]` — mainline `spinand_init_flash()` carries

### New leading hypothesis: the 3V3 rail collapses during program/erase `[derived]`

Reads are byte-perfect and endlessly repeatable. Erase and program are the only operations that run
the chip's internal charge pump, and they are the only ones that draw the datasheet's 25 mA. A
high-resistance power or ground contact — the kind that reads fine on a meter and still drops
hundreds of millivolts under load — browns the chip out mid-operation. The chip acknowledges the
command, the pump stalls, and the block silently keeps its old contents. Every observation above is
that story, and none of it is the story of a corrupted SPI bus.

**The question for Peter: when the wires were replaced, were 3V3 and GND replaced too, or only the
data pair?** The old harness had at least one open conductor; a power lead from the same batch with
a marginal crimp is the obvious suspect, and it would have been invisible to every test run so far.

## ROOT CAUSE: every ODD page of a multi-page write is silently skipped `[measured]`

The new harness did not fix it either — so the fault was never the wiring, the power or the part.
It is in the write path on this platform, and it is exactly reproducible.

### The measurement

Erase a block, write N pages in one call, read back, compare page by page:

| pages written | pages that landed |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1 of 1 |
| 2 | 1 of 2 |
| 4 | 2 of 4 |
| 8 | 4 of 8 |
| 16 | 8 of 16 |
| 64 | 33 of 64 |
| **4 × separate single-page calls** | **4 of 4** |

And which pages, for the 8-page case:

```
page 0: LANDED    page 1: BLANK
page 2: LANDED    page 3: BLANK
page 4: LANDED    page 5: BLANK
page 6: LANDED    page 7: BLANK
```

**Even pages land with byte-correct data. Odd pages are left erased.** Not corrupted, not
half-written — untouched, reading `ff` all the way. A single page written on its own always lands.

### What it is not

- **Not signal integrity.** Identical ratios at 1 MHz and 5 MHz — clock-independent. A timing race
  does not survive a 5× change in timing margin.
- **Not power.** Every wire in the harness has been replaced, and the failure did not move.
- **Not the part.** A single-page write, and four separate single-page writes to consecutive pages,
  round-trip byte-perfect every time — verified with a self-describing pattern, not random data.
- **Not `nandwrite`.** `dd` straight to `/dev/mtd0` reproduces it page for page, so it is below
  userspace: the MTD / `spinand` / `dw_spi` path in this kernel (6.18.39+rpt-rpi-2712).

### Mechanism, offered as a hypothesis not a fact

`[derived]` The signature fits a **doubled page stride in the multi-page write loop**: the data
pointer and the flash offset both advance by two pages per iteration, so even pages receive the
right bytes at the right address and odd pages are never issued at all. That explains why the
landed pages match *their own* expected content rather than their neighbour's — an address-doubling
bug would have put page 1's data at page 2 and produced mismatches, and it does not.

### Why this looked like four different faults all afternoon

Every earlier symptom now reduces to this one cause. `ubiformat` wrote EC and VID headers into
adjacent pages, so roughly half of them never reached the flash — hence "185 eraseblocks have valid
erase counter" after a run that reported writing 1024, hence PEB 2's stale image sequence number
surviving three formats, hence "erased PEB 161, but a non-0xFF byte found", and hence 2.8 million
byte mismatches in `nandtest`. One bug, wearing four costumes.

## RETRACTION: it is NOT a kernel bug. Short back-to-back SPI transactions are unreliable.

**I said the alternating page loss was a bug in the kernel write path and that the hardware was
exonerated. That was wrong**, and the disproof is my own: I wrote a driver by hand over `spidev`,
bypassing the kernel's NAND stack completely, and **it reproduces the failure exactly.**

### What the kernel source actually says

`spinand_mtd_write()` iterates with `nanddev_io_for_each_page`, and `nanddev_io_iter_next_page()`
advances the position by one page and the buffer by `datalen`. No doubling. `spinand_write_page()`
is textbook: `write_enable -> write_to_cache -> program -> spinand_wait() -> check STATUS_PROG_FAILED`.
The rpi-6.18.y tree differs from mainline only by the newer `spinand_fill_*_op` refactor being
absent. **There is no stride bug. My hypothesis was wrong.**

### Driving the chip by hand — the same failure

Eight consecutive pages, explicit WRITE ENABLE / PROGRAM LOAD / PROGRAM EXECUTE / poll-until-ready:

```
page 0: WRONG (reads back all zeros)   page 1: LANDED
page 2: BLANK                          page 3: LANDED
page 4: BLANK                          page 5: LANDED
page 6: BLANK                          page 7: LANDED
```

And the status register came back as `0x00` on one program and `0xF8` / `0xFC` / `0xE0` on the next
— values with P-FAIL, both ECC bits and LUT-F set at once, which is not a plausible chip state.
**The status reads themselves are unreliable.**

### The measurement that settles it

Insert a delay between every SPI transaction:

```
delay 0        pages: ? L _ L _ L _ L   -> 4 of 8
delay 500 us   pages: L L L L L L L L   -> 8 of 8
delay 2 ms     pages: L L L L L L L L   -> 8 of 8
```

Then narrow it to a delay in ONE place only — between `PROGRAM EXECUTE` and the first status poll:

```
init-delay 0 us     BUSY seen on 4/8 programs   -> 4 of 8
init-delay 10 us    BUSY seen on 4/8            -> 4 of 8
init-delay 50 us    BUSY seen on 3/8            -> 4 of 8
init-delay 100 us   BUSY seen on 1/8            -> 4 of 8
init-delay 300 us   BUSY seen on 0/8            -> 8 of 8
```

**At 300 us the program has already finished before anyone looks, BUSY is never observed, and every
page lands.** Below that, the poll sometimes sees BUSY, sometimes sees nonsense, and half the pages
are lost. A driver that trusts the BUSY flag — which is every correct driver, including the
kernel's — proceeds early, and the next page's WRITE ENABLE arrives at a chip that is still
programming and is discarded.

### What this points at `[derived]`

The operations that survive are the ones separated by a long transaction: `PROGRAM LOAD` clocks
2048 bytes and takes 16 ms at 1 MHz, and it never fails. The operations that fail are **short
transactions issued back-to-back** — EXECUTE then status, status then the next WRITE ENABLE.
Isolated short transactions are fine, which is why the JEDEC ID probe, the status dump and every
single-page write have always worked perfectly.

So this is an electrical or chip-select problem on rapid consecutive transactions, not a software
one. Candidates, in the order I would test them:

1. **The chip-select line.** GPIO8 is a software-driven GPIO CS on this overlay. A CS that does not
   cleanly rise and settle between two transactions issued microseconds apart would do exactly this,
   and would be invisible to every test that issues one transaction at a time.
2. **Ground return quality** at high transaction rates — still flying leads, still no ground plane.
3. The part itself, which remains possible but is now the least likely of the three: it answers
   perfectly whenever it is given room.

### Current state of the box

`dtoverlay=w25n01gv` is **commented out** in `config.txt` and `/dev/spidev0.0` is back, because
hand-driving the part is how this gets diagnosed. `mtd0` and UBIFS are therefore not present. One
line un-commented and a reboot restores them.

## FIXED — `spi-cs-inactive-delay-ns` `[measured]`

The chip-select theory was right. Added to the flash node in the overlay:

```dts
spi-cs-inactive-delay-ns = <300000>;   /* tPROG-sized gap around every CS */
spi-cs-setup-delay-ns    = <1000>;
spi-cs-hold-delay-ns     = <1000>;
```

Both failures disappear, through the **kernel** path this time, not a hand-written one:

| test | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| 64-page write | 33 of 64 pages landed | **64 of 64** |
| 16-page write | 8 of 16 | **16 of 16** |
| 8-block batch erase | 2 of 8 erased | **8 of 8** |

And the thing this was all for:

```
ubi0:aerovault  110M  24K  106M  1% /mnt/aerovault

Logical eraseblock size:                126976 bytes, 124.0 KiB
Total amount of logical eraseblocks:    1022 (123.7 MiB)
Count of bad physical eraseblocks:      2
Count of reserved physical eraseblocks: 18
Minimum input/output unit size:         2048 bytes
Volume ID: 0  name: aerovault  size: 1000 LEBs (121.0 MiB)  State: OK
```

**UBIFS is mounted at `/mnt/aerovault`, 106 MiB free of a 128 MiB part** — the rest is UBI's own
overhead plus 18 reserved eraseblocks for bad-block handling, which is the pool that makes NAND
survivable. Two physical eraseblocks are bad, well inside the datasheet's `Nvb` 1004-of-1024.

### What the fault actually was

Short SPI transactions issued back-to-back on this board are unreliable: the chip's BUSY flag reads
back clear while it is still programming, and a status byte read in that window comes back as
nonsense. Every correct driver trusts BUSY, so every correct driver proceeds too early and loses the
next operation. Forcing a gap around each chip-select assertion removes the window entirely.

**This is a workaround, not a repair.** The underlying problem is still flying leads with no
ground plane, and the delay is costing real throughput. The honest statement is: *the system works,
and it works because it is being slowed to the point where the marginal link cannot fail.*

### Open

29. **The 300 us figure is measured, not derived, and is almost certainly far larger than needed.**
    It came from the bench sweep (100 us failed, 300 us worked) with the part running at 1 MHz.
    Walk both numbers back: raise `spi-max-frequency` toward the part's rated 104 MHz and reduce
    `spi-cs-inactive-delay-ns`, re-running the burst bisect at each step, and keep the fastest
    combination that gives 64 of 64. Everything needed to do that is committed.
30. **The right fix is copper, not delay.** Short leads with a ground return beside each signal, or
    a proper adapter board, would likely let this run at full speed with no CS delay at all. Worth
    doing before anything depends on this part's throughput.

## Survives a reboot — but not the way the documentation suggests `[measured]`

**The obvious approach fails, and the boot log says why in two lines:**

```
[2.415] UBI error: cannot open mtd 0, error -19       <- ubi module loaded here
[3.468] spi-nand spi0.0: Winbond SPI NAND was found   <- the flash appeared a second LATER
systemd-modules-load[305]: Failed to insert module 'ubi': No such device
```

`modules-load.d` + `options ubi mtd=0` + an fstab line loads UBI at ~2.4 s, while the SPI NAND is
not probed until the SPI bus is up at ~3.5 s. **Attaching on a timer is a race you lose**, silently:
the module refuses to load, the fstab entry is skipped because of `nofail`, and the Pi boots looking
perfectly healthy with no filesystem.

**Attach on the EVENT instead.** `ubi-persist.sh` installs:

1. `/etc/udev/rules.d/99-aerovault.rules` — on `mtd0` appearing, `ENV{SYSTEMD_WANTS}+="aerovault.service"`
2. `/etc/systemd/system/aerovault.service` — `modprobe ubi` (which attaches via the modprobe
   option), `ubiattach -m 0` as a tolerated fallback, then `mount /mnt/aerovault`
3. `/etc/fstab` — the same volume as `noauto,nofail`, so the service owns the mount and a flash
   fault can never hold up a headless boot

### Proof

A marker file was written **before** the reboot, and checked after one with nothing run by hand:

```
before:  f15ded79f085117776126d3cdf8def698b1b7add9d30730ac871f13176545a83
after :  f15ded79f085117776126d3cdf8def698b1b7add9d30730ac871f13176545a83
ubi0:aerovault on /mnt/aerovault type ubifs (rw,relatime,ubi=0,vol=0)
```

### The attach takes 44 seconds, and that is a design constraint

```
[ 4.143592] ubi0: attaching mtd0
[48.193230] ubi0: good PEBs: 1022, bad PEBs: 2, corrupted PEBs: 0
```

UBI scans all 1024 eraseblocks at attach, and at 1 MHz with a 300 us gap per chip-select that takes
**44 seconds**. `/mnt/aerovault` therefore does not exist until roughly 48 s into boot.

**Anything that writes to AeroVault must order itself `After=aerovault.service`** — a service that
starts earlier and writes to `/mnt/aerovault` will silently write into the root filesystem under the
mountpoint instead, and those writes will vanish from view the moment the real mount lands. That is
a nasty failure and it is entirely avoidable.

It is also the sharpest argument yet for open items 29 and 30: at the part's rated speed this scan
would be under a second.

## Tuning attempt: the clock CANNOT come up on this wiring. Reverted. `[measured]`

Peter asked to tune the clock back up. The answer is no, and it cost the part eight spare blocks to
establish. Recording it in full because the failure mode is the dangerous kind — it passes the

### What the bench sweep said (and why it was not enough)

Driving the part by hand over `spidev`, 8 then 16 then 256 pages per cell:

```
clock            2us     20us     50us    100us
    5 MHz      PASS     PASS     PASS     PASS
   10 MHz      PASS     PASS     PASS     PASS
   25 MHz      PASS     PASS     PASS     PASS
   40 MHz      PASS     PASS     PASS     PASS
   50 MHz      0/16     0/16     0/16     0/16      <- hard cliff between 45 and 50 MHz
```

On that evidence 25 MHz with a 20 us gap looked like a comfortable choice: an octave under the
cliff, ten times over the minimum gap.

### What the real test said

| setting | 64-page test | full 16 MiB write + read + remount | blocks consumed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 MHz / 20 us | **64 of 64** | **FAILED** — `UBIFS error: bad node type (255 but expected 1)`, read returned `EIO` | — |
| 10 MHz / 100 us | 64 of 64 | checksum **passed** | **9 PEBs marked bad in one pass** |
| 1 MHz / 300 us | 64 of 64 | passed, `corrupted PEBs: 0`, no blocks marked | none |

The 10 MHz row is the one to fear. It **passes**: same sha256 written, re-read cold and re-read
across an unmount. Underneath, the kernel log reads

```
ubi0 error: ubi_io_write: error -5 while writing 2048 bytes to PEB 436:36864, written 0 bytes
ubi0 error: ubi_io_sync_erase: erased PEB 436, but a non-0xFF byte found
ubi0: mark PEB 436 as bad
```

UBI is doing its job — retrying elsewhere and retiring the block — so the data is correct and the
test is green. But a NAND part has a finite spare pool, and this one has **18 reserved blocks**.
Nine went in a single 16 MiB pass. At that rate the flash is exhausted in well under a gigabyte of
writes, and the only visible symptom until then is that everything works.

**Bad blocks over the course of this tuning: 2 -> 10.** Eight of the eighteen spares are gone, and
they do not come back. That is real, permanent damage to this part, caused by my tuning run.

### Reverted

Back to `spi-max-frequency = <1000000>` and `spi-cs-inactive-delay-ns = <300000>`, rebuilt from the
committed overlay (the Pi's copy and the repo's had drifted apart during the sweep — my fault, and
worth avoiding: edit the repo file, copy it over, never `sed` the remote copy).

### The lesson, which is the general one

**A 64-page write test is not an acceptance test for a flash device.** It passed at every setting
tried, including one that corrupts data and one that silently eats the spare pool. The acceptance
test has to be (a) large enough to be statistical — 8192 pages, not 64 — and (b) instrumented on
`/sys/class/mtd/mtd0/bad_blocks` and `dmesg | grep "mark PEB"` either side, because **UBI's job is
to hide exactly the failure you are testing for.**

I also had a broken instrument mid-sweep: `timing-sweep.py` erased only the first block of a
multi-block page range, so writes past page 63 landed on un-erased pages and it reported a confident
`128/256`. Fixed, and worth remembering that the wrong answer was a clean, plausible-looking
fraction.
