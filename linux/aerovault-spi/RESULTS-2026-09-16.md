## The part is not there `[measured]`

```
JEDEC ID (9Fh + dummy) : 00 00 00   expected EF AA 21
```

Then the discriminating test — enable the Pi's internal pull-up on MISO (GPIO9) and read again:

```
JEDEC ID (9Fh + dummy) : FF FF FF
```

**MISO is undriven.** A powered W25N01GV with CS asserted would drive that line during the ID read
and return `EF AA 21` regardless of the pull-up; instead the line simply follows whatever the Pi
does with it — 0x00 floating, 0xFF pulled up. So: the click board is **not connected, not powered,
or its DO line is open.** Pull-up restored to `pn` afterwards.

**Control, so this is a finding and not a broken instrument:** the same bus, same session, same
`spidev0.0` — `SPI_LOOP` internal loopback still echoes byte-clean to 50 MHz. The controller, the
driver, the ioctl path and the CS are all working. Only the part is missing.

### CORRECTION to this morning's entry

I wrote that the pull-up test showed **"MISO is open."** That was over-stated and I am retracting
the *specific* claim. The W25N's `DO` pin is high-Z whenever `/CS` is high, so a correctly wired,
powered part would ALSO let GPIO9 follow the Pi's internal pull — the test cannot tell an open wire
from a silent chip. What is proven, now that CS is confirmed asserting, is the weaker and more
useful statement: **during a read with `/CS` low, nothing drives MISO.** Which wire or which rail is
at fault is not determined from here.

I also briefly recorded "CS never goes low" from a 14-sample run. **That was my instrument, not the
Pi** — the samples all completed in ~0.2 s while the Python transfer process was still importing
`spidev`, so every sample landed before the bus was busy. Sampling during the transfer gives
200/200 low. Exactly the §3 failure mode: a broken measurement that agreed with what I suspected.

### For Peter, in order of likelihood

1. **GND.** The most common flying-lead miss, and it fails exactly like this.
2. **3V3 to the click board.** Flash 5 Click is a 3.3 V part; mikroBUS also carries 5 V, and the
   3V3 rail is the one this needs.
3. **`SDI`/`SDO` crossed.** `[assumed]` — mikroBUS silkscreens name data lines from the *click
   board's* point of view, so its `SDI` takes the Pi's **MOSI** (GPIO10, pin 19) and its `SDO`
   drives the Pi's **MISO** (GPIO9, pin 21). Reading those labels as if they were the Pi's is the
   classic way to cross them, and a crossed pair produces precisely this symptom — the part never
   receives a command, so it never drives anything back.
4. CS → GPIO8 (pin 24), SCK → GPIO11 (pin 23). Both confirmed live from the Pi side.

### Two physical tests that will actually settle it

**A. Loop the harness at its far end.** Disconnect the click board and short the MOSI and MISO
wires *to each other at the click-board end* of the cable, then:
```
ssh node@aeronode.local 'python3 /tmp/spidev-loopback.py --expect wired'
```
Echo = every centimetre of copper and every connector contact from the Pi header to the board is
good, and the fault is the board or its supply. All-zero = the harness is the fault, and this says
so without any guessing about which end.

**B. Prove the board has power.** A meter across the click board's own 3V3 and GND pads should read
3.3 V. With no meter: run a wire from the click board's 3V3 pad to Pi header **pin 22 (GPIO25)** —
3.3 V into a Pi input is safe — and `pinctrl get 25` read from here says `hi` if the board is
powered and `lo` if it is not.

### MISO/MOSI swapped — still `00 00 00`, so the data lines are not the fault

Peter swapped the two data wires. **Both orientations now measured, both silent.** That is
informative: a crossed data pair was the leading hypothesis and it is now eliminated by experiment,
whichever way round the original was.

Pull test repeated with the board attached — and it reads **identically to when nothing was
attached**:

| pin | pull-up | pull-down |
|---|---|---|
| GPIO9 | hi | lo |
| GPIO10 | hi | lo |

Both pins follow the Pi's own ~50 kΩ pulls exactly. That pull is weak enough that this is not proof
on its own, but it is what an open line looks like, and it is not what a powered part clamping the
line through its ESD diodes looks like.

**Stop swapping wires.** Two swaps have cost two round trips and eliminated one hypothesis. What is
left — power, ground, or contact — cannot be distinguished by permuting signals, only by measuring.
Tests A and B above, in that order.

### 3V3 and GND confirmed present — so sweep the whole header for stray rail connections

Peter confirms six wires: SPI plus 3V3 plus GND, direct to the Pi, no mikroBUS socket. Probe still
`00 00 00`.

New instrument: `gpio-rail-sweep.sh` drives every unclaimed GPIO to input, pulls it high then low,
and reports any pin that **refuses to follow** — a floating pin follows the pull, a pin strapped to
a rail does not. It touches only pins with no alt function and no driver consumer, and restores
them afterwards.

Result — **exactly one anomaly on the entire 40-pin header** `[measured]`:

```
GPIO6   pu=lo  pd=lo   *** HELD LOW — tied to GND ***
GPIO7, 12, 13, 16, 17, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27   floating — nothing attached
```

**GPIO6 is physical pin 31, and it is hard-grounded.** Physical pin 31 sits directly beside pin 30,
which *is* a ground. That is the classic off-by-one when counting a header by hand.

Two things this sweep does and does not establish:

- **It rules out a stray 3V3.** No pin anywhere on the header reads HELD HIGH, so the 3V3 wire is
  not sitting on a GPIO by mistake — it is on a real 3V3 pin, or on nothing.
- **It cannot find a misplaced SIGNAL wire.** The click board's SPI pins are high-Z when idle, so a
  data wire on the wrong free GPIO still reads "floating". Only rail straps are visible this way.

### The question that settles it faster than any further measurement

**Which PHYSICAL pin numbers are the six wires on?** The BCM numbers map to physical pins that look
nothing like them:

| signal | BCM | physical pin |
|---|---|---|
| MOSI | GPIO10 | **19** |
| MISO | GPIO9 | **21** |
| SCLK | GPIO11 | **23** |
| /CS | GPIO8 | **24** |

Counting physical pins 8/9/10/11 instead of BCM 8/9/10/11 lands on GPIO14, **a ground**, GPIO15 and

## THE FAULT IS THE HARNESS, not the part `[measured]`

Peter shorted `SDI` to `SDO` at the click board's own header. The loop does not close.

```
0.1 / 1 / 5 / 10 / 25 / 50 MHz   ->  ALL-ZERO at every clock
POSITIVE CONTROL FAIL — no echo at any clock
```

And then the same question asked with **DC only — no SPI, no clock, no timing, nothing that can be
blamed on a driver**: GPIO10 re-muxed to a plain output, GPIO9 to a plain input.

| MOSI driven | MISO pull | MISO reads |
|---|---|---|
| LOW | up | **hi** |
| HIGH | up | **hi** |
| LOW | down | **lo** |
| HIGH | down | **lo** |

**MISO ignores MOSI completely and follows only the Pi's own internal pull.** With the two pads
shorted at the far end, a working harness would force MISO to track MOSI in all four rows. There is
no conductive path from the Pi header to the board and back.

That closes the question the whole afternoon has been circling: **the Pi is fine, the driver is
fine, the chip select is fine, and the part was never the suspect it looked like.** Every `00 00 00`
is explained by copper that does not conduct. Pins restored to `a0` (SPI0) afterwards.

Caveat worth stating: "no path" also covers "the short at the board is not actually shorting". The
next test distinguishes that too.

### Which wire — one more short, and the answer falls out

Short **`SDI`, `SDO` and `GND` all three together** at the click board's header. Then, from here,
both data pins are set to inputs with pull-ups and read:

| GPIO9 (MISO) | GPIO10 (MOSI) | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| lo | lo | all three wires conduct — the earlier short was the problem, not the wires |
| lo | **hi** | the **MOSI** wire is open |
| **hi** | lo | the **MISO** wire is open |
| hi | hi | the **GND** wire is open (a common-mode failure needs a common cause) |

Cheapest fix if these are DuPont jumpers: replace the lot before measuring further. A crimp that
looks seated and is not is the single most common cause of exactly this signature.

### New wires — the harness now conducts `[measured]`

With the replacement jumpers fitted and the `SDI`/`SDO` short still on the board:

```
MOSI driven low  -> MISO reads lo
MOSI driven high -> MISO reads hi
```

MISO tracks MOSI exactly, by DC, through the full length of the harness and the board's own header.
**The copper is good.** The old wires were the fault, and the loop test found it.

The probe still reads `00 00 00`, and that is now fully explained: **the diagnostic short is still
fitted.** With `SDI` tied to `SDO`, the part's data output is strapped to the Pi's data output, so
nothing can talk — and while the chip drives `DO` it is fighting the Pi's `MOSI` driver, which is
contention that should not be left running. Remove the short, then probe.
