EKF Standing Drift

Six thousand position estimates from a flight controller that never moved. The cloud below is what the navigation filter believed about its own location over 150 seconds on a bench.

2.45 mmax drift

The GPS was reporting a 3D fix with 21 satellites and a horizontal accuracy of 0.73 m. The filter's own estimate wandered 2.45 m from its centroid in the same window — more than three times worse than the receiver feeding it. That is not a GPS problem. EKF3 was running in CONST_POS_MODE: with no calibrated compass there is no usable yaw reference, so it declined to fuse GPS horizontally at all and coasted on dead reckoning from an uncalibrated accelerometer.

The reported variances look excellent — velVar 0.00119, posVar 0.00149. They are not evidence of a good solution. In constant-position mode the position variance is small because the position is held, not because it is known. The cloud above is what that confidence is actually worth.

Source: ArduPlane on --board=pi5, AP_HAL_Linux, stock driver. Position and attitude logged from the EKF at 40 Hz; ENU computed about the run centroid. Every figure on this page is measured, not modelled.
The same track is provided as ahrs_drift_track.geojson (3D CRS84) and ahrs_drift_track.kml (absolute altitude) for ArcGIS Pro, ArcGIS Online and ArcGIS Earth.
Caveat worth keeping: the accelerometer is uncalibrated — it reads 10.28 m/s² against g = 9.807, a 4.8% scale error, with a 0.217 m/s² offset on X. Calibrate, then re-run this and the cloud should collapse.
